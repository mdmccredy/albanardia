## How this Bible works

This Bible is the settled canon for Albanardia. The Figma files stay as idea boards: messy on purpose, full of drafts and alternatives. When something is decided on a board, it moves here.

- **Canon:** anything written in the sections below.
- **Undecided:** anything in Open questions. These are forks, not mistakes.
- **Boards:** [Silverbrooks, Albanardia, United States](https://www.figma.com/design/X5kFw3vEXFARRv886tiZPJ/Silverbrooks--Albanardia--United-States) and [Colleges in Albanardia State](https://www.figma.com/design/biu1j5IDYthE2dINREHcEx/Colleges-in-Albanardia-State).

**Brand rule (canon v100).** Albanardia uses real-world companies by default: banks, stores, airlines, stadium sponsors and chains are the real ones (BECU Stadium, jetBlue Park, AmEx Garden, Wawa, Chipotle and so on). Max's own invented companies are used only where stated. Current list of Max's companies: Larssen Sailing (sails and dinghies: SK400, Pulse XL, Bauer), Nysel Boatworks, MC Performance Yachts (MC42), Kwáani Airlines, Asia Pacific Airlines (APA), Vistarre Airlines, Lishian Airways, Pacific Airways, Port of Silverbrooks, Silverbrooks Ballet, Garnett Center's Alexandria Hall, Rutherford Public Library and The Silverbrooks Times.

**What counts as canon.** Anything Max states or draws (Figma boards, Procreate maps, logos) is canon. Details Claude invents for flavor (news stories, scores, statistics in the newspapers) are not canon unless Max confirms them.

## Silverbrooks

Silverbrooks (SB) is the state capital and biggest city, in Thompson County on Albanardia Bay. The Wellington River flows in from the west between Westbrook/Château-du-Pierre and Ulvaeus, past SBU and AIED, into the inner harbor. That inner harbor, between downtown and Pearl's End, is Pearl's End Bay; the outer harbor, between downtown and East Harbor opening onto the bay, is Silverbrooks Harbor; Samolette Harbor lies north of SBX. On the north shore, the cove between Silverbrooks and Whilliker is Hanson Bay, home to the Hanson Bay Marina: one compact block of \~1,200 slips on parallel docks along the bay's south shore, sheltered by a long offshore breakwater (like Seattle's Shilshole Bay Marina), with Silverbrooks Yacht Club at its east end.

## Silverbrooks culture

People from Silverbrooks are Silverbrookians, or "Brookers" among themselves. Think Bostonians, but sharper and more polished: the same pride, loyalty and bluntness, with more brains and better style. As a group, they call themselves the Northeasterns.

- **Silverbrookian vs Bostonian:** same fierce city pride, sports obsession and harbor history; Silverbrookians are more educated-sounding, better dressed and less rowdy about it.
- **Gossip culture:** polite to your face, full commentary once you've left the room. Talking about people behind their backs is practically a city pastime.

* **Identity:** "Northeastern" is on everything: the Northeastern Transit Authority, the hockey league, the sailing foundation, the yacht club.
* **SB everywhere:** SBMetro, SBTrain, SB Voyageurs; saying "SB" instead of Silverbrooks is normal.
* **Personality:** judgey on first contact and famous for resting unimpressed face, but genuinely kind and loyal once they know you. Outsiders read them as cold; locals call it honest.
* **On the water:** sailing is ordinary, not fancy. Kids learn at community sailing centers, and summer weekends revolve around AYRC regattas.
* **Daily rhythm:** a Swedish-style afternoon coffee break, French bakeries on every corner, Lishian market lunches.
* **Transit city:** most people ride SBMetro, which everyone just calls "the Metro"; owning a car downtown is seen as a hassle.
* **Signs and speech:** welcome signs in English, French, Swedish and Holdenish; the accent sounds Bostonian with a Swedish lilt and French softness*.*
* **Rivalries:** SB vs Albanardia City in everything, from college sports to which city has the better harbor.
* **Seasons:** Midsummer on Midsommar Green, Bastille Day on Promenade Saint-Bruno, winter hockey at McKay Garden.

## The Harbor Artery Project ("the Long Dig")

Silverbrooks' version of Boston's Big Dig: the megaproject that tore down the elevated Harbor Viaduct and put I-95 below the city.

| Piece | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| I-95 Downtown Tunnel | I-95 runs underground beneath Jude-Mayhen and downtown |
| I-95 Harbor Tunnel | I-95 continues under Pearl's End Bay and surfaces in Pearl's End |
| Artery Park | A long, narrow strip park running directly on top of the Downtown Tunnel, like Boston's Rose Kennedy Greenway |

I-47 does not enter downtown: it merges into I-95 at Exit 138. I-285 splits off I-95 at Exit 140 and loops around the west side to rejoin it north of the city.

Locals still complain about how long it took and what it cost, hence the nickname.

## History of Silverbrooks (timeline)

Silverbrooks was built by three waves of people, and you can still read them in the street map. **Swedes** came first, with New Sweden in the 1640s: the oldest farm names (Nygård, Farsgod, Ulvaeus) and the Lutheran churches are theirs. **French Huguenots** arrived from the 1680s on, after France outlawed Protestantism, and named the parishes north and south of the river (Saint-Bruno, Saint-Paris, Château-du-Pierre) and worked the oyster beds at Pearl's End. In the 1800s, **Holdenish and Lishian** immigrants came through the harbor by the tens of thousands to work the mills and the docks; Holdena and Lishia are North Sea countries in this world, and their markets, music halls and surnames (Lindgren, Jude-Mayhen) fill the inner neighborhoods. English, German and Italian families followed later. That mix is why the city has Swedish, French and English street names side by side, and why the accent sounds the way it does.

| Year | Event |
| --- | --- |
| 1640s | Swedish and Finnish settlers from the New Sweden colony build a trading post and farms where the Wellington River meets the bay; the shallow, silver-looking creeks give the settlement its name. Nygård ("new farm") and Farsgod are their farm names |
| 1655–1664 | The Dutch, then the English, take over New Sweden. The Swedish farmers stay, keep their land and their Lutheran parish |
| 1680s–1720s | French Huguenot families, refugees after France outlaws Protestantism in 1685, settle what become Saint-Bruno, Saint-Paris and Château-du-Pierre; Pearl's End grows around the oyster beds |
| 1700s | Silverbrooks becomes a shipbuilding and oyster port; the ropewalks and sail lofts give Ropewalk Ave and Sailmaker Ln their names |
| 1776 | The Lower Counties on the Bay break from Pennsylvania as the State of Albanardia |
| 1787 | Albanardia ratifies the Constitution; Silverbrooks becomes the state capital |
| 1790s | Silverbrooks Public Library founded, the third oldest library system in the US; Silverbrooks University chartered in 1799 |
| 1810s–1840s | Water-powered mills rise along the Wellington River (Old Mill, Ulvaeus) |
| 1838 | The Silverbrooks Times prints its first issue |
| 1840s–1890s | Holdenish and Lishian immigrants arrive through the harbor by the tens of thousands, filling Jude-Mayhen, Lindgren Heights and the mill neighborhoods |
| 1861–1865 | Albanardia stays with the Union; the East Harbor navy yard builds and repairs warships |
| 1880s–1900s | Elliot McKay leads the city out of a chaotic, corrupt machine era: a professional police force, clean water, parks and public transit. His name ends up on the university, McKay Field, McKay Boulevard and the medical center |
| 1897 | NETA opens the first stretch of the A Line between Elliot Square and Saint-Bruno, one of the first subways in the country |
| 1904–1912 | The A Line tunnels under Pearl's End Bay; the B Line opens in 1908 as an elevated line on Westbrook Ave; the A reaches Whilliker in 1912 |
| 1925 | The C Line opens across the Wellington River |
| 1930s | McKay Field opens as the city's first airport; the Westbrook El is buried as a subway in 1931 |
| 1959 | The elevated Harbor Viaduct carries I-95 through downtown, cutting the city off from its waterfront |
| 1968–1974 | Silverbrooks Thompson International (SBX) is built on fill in the bay, with the D Line under the harbor |
| 1991–2008 | The Harbor Artery Project ("the Long Dig") replaces the viaduct with the Downtown Tunnel and the Harbor Tunnel |
| 2000s | East Harbor Center, Alexandria Hall and the new Silverbrooks Ballet building (Nordfors Center) open downtown; the G Line streetcar starts in 2006 |
| 2009 | Artery Park opens on top of the Downtown Tunnel |
| 2010s | The Samolette Container Terminal opens, making the Port of Silverbrooks one of the biggest on the East Coast; the driverless E Line opens in 2012; Hanson Bay Marina is rebuilt beside SBYC |
| 2020s | Silverbrooks passes 1,000,000 people |

## Transportation

Four highways, three airports, and one big regional transit authority, NETA, tie the state together.

**Highways**

| Road | Route |
| --- | --- |
| I-95 | Runs the full length of the state (\~231 mi of I-95 in Albanardia), then south over the Strait of Washington. Goes below grade through Silverbrooks as part of the Harbor Artery Project ("the Long Dig"): the I-95 Downtown Tunnel under downtown, then the I-95 Harbor Tunnel under Pearl's End Bay. Exits are numbered by mile from the Virginia line |
| I-47 | Runs from Albanardia City across the state to Silverbrooks, and merges into I-95 at Exit 138, just south of the I-285 split; it does not enter downtown |
| I-285 | Western bypass of Silverbrooks (like I-405 around Seattle): leaves I-95 at Exit 140 in Thompson Flats, runs north past Evansville and over the Wellington River, then hugs Silverbrooks' northwest edge and rejoins I-95 between Whilliker and SB at Exit 161. Along that stretch I-285 is the Silverbrooks city line: everything outside it belongs to the suburbs. Carscaddon Avenue links into it near Westbrook |
| Highway 53 | US 53, the Atlantic Coast Highway, down the whole east side; it starts at I-95 Exit 133 southwest of Silverbrooks and runs east past SBX before heading south along the coast |

**Carscaddon Avenue (Highway 531):** a mid-size divided city highway, Silverbrooks' version of Seattle's Aurora Ave. It runs about 29 miles from McKay Field, south past Evansville and across I-285, then southeast through the city as the Silverbrooks / Marisfield line (Ulvaeus and Old Mill on the SB side; Belmont and Thompson Flats on the Marisfield side), ending at US 53 by the airport. It has traffic lights and cross streets instead of exits.

**Northwest state highways:** rural two-lane routes across Northmark, Archila, Rochester and Moïyane counties.

**Silverbrooks main roads:** a hybrid of radial spokes, a light grid, and Boston-style sweeping curves.

- **Radial spokes from downtown:** McKay Boulevard, NE Belwether St, Lindgren Avenue, Westbrook Ave, Saint-Paris Ave, Thompson Rd, SE Foundland St, Harborview Rd, Grand Avenue, Royal Ave, Mariners Blvd, Lyngby Road and Ulvsberg Road. They start at the edge of the old core and bend gently out to the city line.
- **Light arterial grid (about one per mile):** Elliot Ave, Linnea Ave, Lafayette Blvd, Kings St, Fontaine Blvd, Commerce St, Ekman St, Mariner Way, Holdena Blvd, Market St, Customs House St, Saint-Antoine St, Columbia Ave, Sailmaker Ln, Harbor Ave, Lindqvist St, Fyrhus St, Ropewalk Ave.
- **Curving avenues:** Boston-style curving routes. Each one is a chain of long, straight runs (a few blocks each) that bend only slightly at intersections, so up close it reads as straight streets, but zoomed out it forms a sweeping arc around downtown. None closes into a ring, and each ends at another main road, the shore or the city line. Crescent Avenue (\~8 mi) sweeps from Château-du-Pierre up over Lindgren Heights and Nygård, then down through Saint-Bruno to the waterfront. Shorter curves: Château Way, Saint-Bruno Parkway and Bayview Drive.
- **Old-core main streets:** crooked through-streets across the Boston-style core: Royal St, State St, Saint-Honoré St, Old Harbor Rd, Church St, Washington St.
- **Waterfront:** Harbor Drive.
- **Naming rule:** every street uses the American format (St, Ave, Blvd, Road, Way, Drive, Parkway); no "Rue …" or "Boulevard …" names.

## Transit and ports

**Northeastern Transit Authority (NETA)** is the one transit authority for the Silverbrooks area, like the MTA or MBTA. It is one of the oldest in the country and runs bus, metro, train, subway and ferry service from Silverbrooks through Rutherford, Evansville and Whilliker up to Waterloo. The old Thompson, Westminster and Rochester county authorities no longer exist; all of it is NETA now.

**SBTransit** is NETA's umbrella brand: the black "SB" circle on every station, bus stop and ticket. Each mode has its own livery, and every logo is a circle with a letter in the middle, in the same font, so the family reads as one company. Locals say "the Metro" for the buses and "the train" for the subway.

**SBTrain subway.** Seven lettered lines plus the G Line streetcar, 54 stations and about 142 route miles, reaching out to Belwren, Skellefteå and Saint-Sebastien in the north and west and to Rutherford and Douremie in the south. Lines go by letter only, the way SEPTA and New York do it: you take "the A." Station codes are the line letter plus a two-digit number counted from the north or west end (Elliot Square is A07, B09, C01 and D05). The five radial lines meet at **Elliot Square**, the downtown hub, where the 1897 subway started. About 690,000 weekday trips.

Full diagram: [SBTrain Subway Map](https://claude.ai/artifact/EK39RfSf9GBKni1DnfvBPC).

**Elliot Square Station** (1 Elliot Square, 19001) is the city's main transit hub, the Silverbrooks version of South Station or 30th Street Station. About 210,000 people a day.

- **Grand Hall:** the 1912 Beaux-Arts headhouse facing Elliot Square, with a four-faced clock over the main floor, ticket windows, shops and food.
- **Subway:** the A, B, C and D platforms sit on four underground levels below the square, plus the G streetcar stop out front. The 1897 A Line platform is the oldest part of the station.
- **Regional Rail terminal:** an 8-track glass train shed along the station's south side. It is a through station: Waterloo to Rutherford trains run straight through, and Evansville trains end here.
- **Bus terminal:** a 14-bay SBMetro terminal along the north side on Moreau Ct for routes 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 7 and 14 and every express (101 to 150).
- **Entrances:** the Grand Hall doors, the plaza stairs, State St, the bus terminal and Hedlund Pl.
- NETA Headquarters is next door at 20 Elliot Square.

**House addresses and street names:** every street on the map has a name (about 5,700 streets across the state), so any home address can be looked up.

- **Grid towns:** numbered streets run one way (1st St, 2nd St…) and named avenues the other (Maple Ave, Lindqvist Ave, Beaumont Ave…). Silverbrooks' numbered streets go past 200th St.
- **Old core and minor streets:** Boston-style names (Harbor St, Winthrop Pl, North Spring Ter…).
- **Suburban cul-de-sacs:** Ct, Ln, Cir, Loop and Trail names.
- **House numbers:** start at 100 at one end of each street and rise about 100 per block, odd on one side and even on the other, US-style.
- **Format:** "456 15th St, Silverbrooks, AB 19002". Silverbrooks addresses take their neighborhood's ZIP; other towns use the town ZIP.
- **On the map:** typing an address in the search bar or the Directions boxes drops a pin there and works as a start or end point.

## People, culture and language

This stretch of the East Coast is a Scandinavian and French melting pot, drawn mainly from Sweden, France, Holdena and Lishia.

- **Demographics:** American, French, Holdenish, Swedish, English, Lishian, Italian, German
- **Accent:** a Boston-style base (dropped R's, broad vowels) with Swedish sing-song rhythm and French-softened consonants; O sounds pulled back in the throat
- **Signage:** welcome signs in English, French, Swedish and Holdenish (Welcome to / Bienvenue à / Välkommen till / Välkommen tyl, the Holdenish form)
- **French-American hub:** Belwether (renamed from Saint-Hélène)
- **Fictional countries:** Holdena and Lishia, detailed later
